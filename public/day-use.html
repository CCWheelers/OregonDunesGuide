GETTING IN

Park, stage,
then slow down.

Day-use areas range from paved OHV staging lots built for trailers to small picnic areas, lake access, overlooks, and trailheads.

  • Fees and passes: Many Forest Service day-use and OHV staging areas require a site fee or accepted recreation pass. Amounts and payment methods vary—check the exact site before arrival.
  • Hours: Use posted opening, closing, and quiet-hour signs. A site being open does not mean every trail, beach segment, or habitat area is open.
  • Trailers: Arrive early on summer weekends. Use designated unloading areas and keep ramps, turnarounds, and emergency access clear.
  • Dogs: At developed Forest Service recreation sites, keep dogs leashed or otherwise physically restrained. Seasonal wildlife areas may be more restrictive.

THE OHV AREA

Unload ready.
Ride map-first.

An Oregon ATV operating permit is required for machines used on public riding land. Operator requirements depend on age and vehicle class, and the Oregon Dunes sound limit is 93 dB.

01

Permit & education

Display the required operating permit and carry applicable safety education credentials. Confirm youth supervision and training rules.

02

Machine setup

Bring the required whip flag, approved spark arrestor, working lights, brakes, helmets, eye protection, and vehicle-specific safety gear.

03

Current riding map

Download the current official map before service fades. Fences, signs, habitat closures, and designated routes control.

04

Group protocol

Name a lead and sweep, agree on hand signals, regroup at every decision point, and keep the first loop short.

Stage before you ride.

Unload only where permitted. Do not operate an unregistered OHV on public roads or ride between a lodging property and the dunes unless the route and vehicle are legal for that connection.

BEYOND THE THROTTLE

Quiet sand,
lakes, and long views.

Some of the most memorable Oregon Dunes day trips never enter an OHV area. These places offer hiking, picnicking, fishing, paddling, photography, and wildlife watching.

WALK TO THE OPEN DUNES

Oregon Dunes Day Use Area

South of Florence, this non-motorized access offers sweeping dune views and a walking route toward the beach. Expect soft-sand effort and wind exposure.

LAKE & FOREST

Alder Dune / Dune Lake

North of Florence, this year-round site connects to Alder Dune Trail, with picnic tables and lake-oriented recreation.

BIG-DUNE HIKE

John Dellenback Dunes Trail

Near Lakeside, a forest approach opens onto a dramatic non-motorized dune landscape. Carry water and navigation for the featureless open sand.

BEACH, WETLAND & WILDLIFE

South Jetty Road pullouts

Separate beach lots, wetland viewpoints, and a crab-dock area make it possible to build a mixed-interest day without entering an OHV zone.

POSTED AT THE COAST

Read the surf.
Respect the edge.

The Pacific is cold, powerful, and changeable. Tide predictions help with planning, but they do not account for every wave, storm, current, river flow, or drifting log.

01

Sneaker waves

Unexpected waves can run much farther up the beach than the previous sets. Never turn your back on the ocean or sit on driftwood near the wash line.

02

Incoming tide

Water can remove a legal wet-sand travel corridor and trap riders against soft sand, logs, or dune faces. Keep a clear exit behind you.

03

Cold water & currents

Oregon surf can cause rapid cold-water incapacitation. Swim only where appropriate, supervise children closely, and understand rip-current escape.

A SIMPLE DAY PLAN

Leave room for
the coast to change.

Wind, fog, tide, closures, and crowding can reshuffle a good plan. Keep one primary objective and one nearby alternative.

EARLY

Arrive and inspect

Park before the busiest window, read every posted sign, pay the site fee, and inspect the staging or trail conditions.

MORNING

Use the best window

Ride the primary loop, take the exposed hike, or use favorable tide and wind conditions while energy and visibility are best.

MIDDAY

Reset by the vehicle

Eat, hydrate, check weather, reassess the group, and choose a shorter second outing.

AFTERNOON

Finish with margin

Return before light or weather deteriorates, clean the staging space, secure equipment, and check every trailer connection.

VERIFY BEFORE ARRIVAL

The sign wins.
The map decides.

Fees, closures, road conditions, fire restrictions, and wildlife boundaries can change. Use this guide to choose an area, then verify the final details with the managing agencies.
