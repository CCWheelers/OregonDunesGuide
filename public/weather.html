DUNE WEATHER FIELD NOTES

What the numbers
mean on sand.

Weather changes traction, visibility, body temperature, and how quickly a simple recovery becomes serious.

01

Wind & gusts

Wind reshapes crests and can erase tracks. Strong crosswinds increase fatigue and make regrouping harder. Reduce speed before exposed ridges.

02

Rain & wet sand

Light rain can firm some sand, but storms reduce visibility and create colder conditions. Waterproof layers belong in every vehicle.

03

Fog & marine cloud

Dense fog removes visual landmarks quickly. Shorten loops, keep the group tight, and use offline navigation instead of following old tracks.

04

Cold exposure

Wind makes mild temperatures feel colder, especially after rain or sweat. Carry a dry insulating layer even on summer afternoons.

LOCAL WEATHER PERSONALITIES

Twenty miles can
change the answer.

Town readings are useful reference points, but exposure, tree cover, fog, and distance from the water create noticeable differences across the dunes.

FLORENCE · NORTH

Lake fog and north-zone wind

Morning marine cloud can linger near lakes and forest edges while South Jetty becomes windier and brighter. Visibility may change within one loop.

WINCHESTER BAY · CENTRAL

Big, exposed sand

The Umpqua Dunes offer less shelter from coastal wind. Gusts can feel stronger on open ridges than at the harbor forecast point.

COOS BAY · SOUTH

Harbor cloud and sheltered pockets

Horsfall and Spinreel can differ from downtown Coos Bay. Forest edges may feel calm while open beach approaches remain windy or foggy.
