    Before the engine
    ever starts.

    These are the items most likely to stop a trip at staging. Permit on the machine, credentials with the operator, legal equipment, and a current map belong together.

    A visible Oregon permit—or a qualifying reciprocal-state permit—is required for machines operated on designated public OHV land.

    01 · PERMITS & PASSES

    The machine permit and the parking pass are different.

    Permit on the machine. Recreation pass in the vehicle.

    ATV operating permit

    Oregon’s ATV permit costs $10 and is valid for two years. It must be displayed visibly on the machine. A plated vehicle still needs the ATV permit when it leaves ordinary roads for trails or dunes.

    Out-of-state permits

    Oregon accepts permits only from states that also accept Oregon permits. Riders from nonreciprocal states must purchase an Oregon permit. Check the current reciprocity list before traveling.

    Recreation passes

    Many Forest Service day-use staging areas and campgrounds require a separate recreation pass, day-use fee, or reservation. An ATV permit does not cover parking or camping.

    Buy or verify an Oregon ATV permit ↗
    02 · KNOW YOUR CLASS

    Oregon regulates four kinds of ATV.

    The rule starts with the vehicle’s legal class—not the nickname riders use.
    CLASS I

    Quads & 3-wheelers

    • ATV permit required
    • Safety Education Card required for every operator
    • Youth rider-fit and supervision rules apply
    CLASS II

    4x4s, trucks & sand rails

    • Valid driving privileges required
    • ATV permit required off road
    • Liability insurance and class-specific equipment required
    CLASS III

    Off-road motorcycles

    • ATV permit required
    • Safety Education Card required for every operator
    • Minimum operator age is seven
    CLASS IV

    Side-by-sides

    • ATV permit required
    • Youth operators under 16 need a Safety Education Card
    • Seats, restraints, and manufacturer age rules apply

    03 · ON THE SAND

    Ride only where motorized travel is designated.

    Read the map, the terrain, and every sign at the same time.
    • Follow the current map and posted signs.Open sand is not automatically open to vehicles. Riding zones, connectors, seasonal boundaries, and closures vary.
    • Alcohol stays in developed sites.Possession of alcohol is prohibited in Forest Service OHV riding areas outside developed campgrounds and day-use sites.
    • Use lights in limited visibility.Headlights and taillights are required before sunrise, after sunset, and whenever you cannot see clearly for 100 feet.
    • Keep every rider in a legal seat.Class II and IV vehicles require a seat and securely mounted seat belt for the operator and each passenger.
    • Honor every closure.Do not enter signed wildlife areas, vegetated closures, nonmotorized areas, or routes closed by an emergency order.
    • Never operate with suspended privileges.A person with a suspended or revoked driver license may not operate any class of ATV in Oregon.
    04 · REQUIRED EQUIPMENT

    Your machine must pass more than a visual check.

    Flag height, sound, exhaust, lighting, restraints, and fire equipment can all matter.
    FLAGRed or orange · 8 × 12 in minimum · 9 ft above ground
    SOUNDOregon Dunes site limit: 93 dB
    EXHAUSTWorking muffler and Forest Service–approved spark arrester
    LIGHTSHeadlight and taillight for darkness or limited visibility
    CLASS IITwo-pound fire extinguisher, roll bar or cab, seats and belts
    VISIONEye protection where required when no windshield is fitted

    Fire-season orders may add a shovel, extinguisher, or other equipment requirement and may restrict travel. Check current Siuslaw National Forest alerts.

    05 · YOUTH RIDERS

    Age, training, fit, and supervision work together.

    Proper fit and active adult supervision are part of the rule—not optional extras.
    • Under 18: helmet required.Operators and passengers must wear a properly fastened DOT-approved helmet, except the limited exemption for a registered street-legal Class II vehicle with a roof or roll bar.
    • Under 16: hands-on training.Youth need the online course plus hands-on training or evaluation, and must be supervised on public land.
    • The supervisor must be qualified.The adult must be at least 18, hold an ATV Safety Education Card, and be able to give immediate assistance and direction.
    • Rider fit is mandatory for Class I.A youth must be able to reach and operate the controls while maintaining a safe riding position.
    • Side-by-sides follow manufacturer age guidance.Class IV operators under 16 may operate only an age-appropriate youth machine.
    • Restraints still apply off road.Youth under 16 must use required seat belts and child restraints in Class II and IV vehicles.
    Review Oregon’s youth rider rules ↗
    06 · ROADS & STAGING

    A sand route and a public road are not the same.

    Rules can change the moment tires leave the riding area.

    Road use can add driver-license, registration, insurance, lighting, restraint, and traffic-law requirements. Out-of-state ATV plates do not automatically authorize highway use in Oregon. Spinreel Road includes a state-highway segment where driving privileges are required.

    Unload only in authorized areas, secure trailers without blocking circulation, and confirm whether your chosen campground allows direct OHV travel between the campsite and the riding network.

    07 · CLOSURES & HABITAT

    The boundary protects wildlife—and continued access.

    A closure line is a hard boundary, even when tracks continue beyond it.

    Western snowy plovers nest on open sand. Seasonal restrictions and signed closures protect nesting, feeding, and resting habitat. Dates and boundaries vary by location, and emergency closures can change access with little notice.

    Do not enter posted closures.Keep pets controlled and follow leash rules.Pack out food and trash that attract predators.Use only designated motorized routes.
